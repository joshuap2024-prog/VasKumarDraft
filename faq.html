Before You Engage

What you need to know first

Whether you are dealing with a dispute, a contract issue, or a business decision with legal implications, here is what clients typically ask before engaging the firm.

Get In Touch

Engaging The Firm

Contact the firm by phone or email with a brief description of your matter and the best time to speak. An initial consultation can be arranged to discuss the issue, review any relevant documents, and give you a clear picture of the legal position.
The consultation is a focused discussion about your matter. You describe the situation, share relevant documents if you have them, and the firm gives you an honest view of the legal position and the available options. It is an opportunity for both sides to assess the matter and how the firm can help.
Costs vary depending on the nature and complexity of the matter. The firm provides a clear cost estimate before work begins so there are no surprises. Fees may be structured on a fixed-fee basis, an hourly rate, or a combination, depending on the type of work involved.
Not necessarily. Many matters can be handled by phone, email, and video conference, particularly in the early stages. In-person meetings at the office are by appointment and are arranged when the nature of the matter makes a face-to-face meeting the better option.

Litigation & Disputes

Bring or send the pleadings, contracts, correspondence, notices, invoices, timelines, and any court or tribunal deadlines. A short summary of the dispute and your preferred outcome is also helpful. Do not worry if your documents are incomplete — the firm can work with what you have.
Often, yes. Depending on the facts, a matter may be handled through negotiation, settlement correspondence, mediation, or other dispute resolution steps before formal litigation is necessary. The firm will assess the most cost-effective and practical route for your situation.
Timelines vary significantly depending on the court, the complexity of the issues, and whether the matter settles before trial. Simpler matters in the Magistrate's Court may resolve within months. More complex cases in the General Division of the High Court can take considerably longer. The firm will give you a realistic timeline assessment for your specific matter.
The firm does not give unfounded assurances about outcomes. What it does is give an honest assessment of the strength of your position based on the facts and the law, the risks involved in proceeding, and what a realistic result might look like. That assessment helps you make a properly informed decision about whether and how to proceed.
In Singapore, costs typically follow the event — the losing party is generally ordered to pay a proportion of the winning party's costs. However, costs awarded by the court rarely cover the full amount of legal fees incurred, and recovery depends on the other party's ability to pay. The firm will explain how costs work in your specific matter.

Corporate & Business Advisory

It can include advice on business agreements, governance issues, commercial risk, regulatory obligations, internal processes, and practical legal steps before a decision or transaction is made. The firm advises business owners, directors, and management teams on decisions with legal consequences.
Yes. The firm can assist with reviewing, drafting, and refining agreements, letters, settlement terms, corporate documents, and other business records that need legal clarity. The focus is on documents that are accurate, enforceable, and clearly understood by all parties.
Yes. Shareholder disputes are a significant part of the firm's practice. This includes disputes over management decisions, profit distributions, oppression claims, and breaches of shareholders' agreements. The firm can advise on resolution options and represent you in negotiations or proceedings as needed.
Contact a lawyer promptly. Do not respond to threats or formal letters of demand without legal advice, as the response can affect your legal position. The firm can review the correspondence, advise you on your position, and help you decide on the most appropriate next step — including whether to engage, negotiate, or defend.
